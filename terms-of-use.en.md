An order is the buyer's offer to the seller to buy the listed items at the price shown when it is sent, with the delivery details the buyer gives. If the seller accepts, a sales contract is concluded between the buyer and the seller only; Oglasino is not a party to it and does not guarantee that it will be carried out (Section 3). Sending an order is not a payment, and Oglasino takes no payment; once the seller accepts, the buyer owes the price to the seller. Where the seller is a trader and the buyer a consumer, that contract is a distance contract under consumer-protection law (Section 14).
- **Payment and delivery.** The buyer pays the seller directly, on delivery or at pickup. We advise buyers not to pay in advance. The price on an order is the price of the items only and does not include the cost of delivery. Delivery, its cost, and its risk are arranged between the buyer and the seller.

- **Sellers receive orders by default.** Every seller's listings can be ordered unless the seller turns off accepting orders in their settings, which they can do at any time. Receiving an order does not oblige the seller to anything: the seller is free to accept or decline it, and an order that is not answered expires.
- **Answering orders.** A seller should accept or decline each order. An order not answered within 48 hours expires. If a seller lets three orders in a row expire, accepting orders is turned off automatically; the seller can turn it on again. A seller should accept only an order they can and intend to fulfil.
- **Cancelling and completing.** The buyer can cancel an order before the seller answers. After acceptance, either party can cancel and must say why. The buyer can confirm receipt; an accepted order that is not cancelled is marked as received 14 days after acceptance.
- **The buyer's details.** The buyer must give true delivery details, and may give another person's details as the recipient only if entitled to. The seller receives the recipient's name, phone, and delivery address only after accepting the order (see the Privacy Policy, Section 2.18).
- **How a seller may use delivery details.** A seller may use the details received with an order only to fulfil and deliver that order, to handle a complaint about it, and to meet a duty the law places on the seller. The seller may pass them to the delivery service that carries the order and to no one else. Any other use — marketing, passing them to others, building a contact list — is prohibited and is grounds for a permanent ban. The seller must not keep the details longer than those purposes need. From the moment the seller receives the details, the seller answers for how they are used; a seller who is a trader handles them as a controller in their own right under data-protection law.

Oglasino only runs the cart and passes the order and its messages between the two. Where the seller is a trader and the buyer a consumer, the trader must themselves meet every duty that consumer-protection law sets for a distance contract (Section 14). In particular, the trader must: keep their business name, registration number, seat address and phone number on their profile, so that the buyer can see them before ordering; tell the buyer the cost of delivery and the total price before accepting the order, in the listing or by message; give the buyer, at the latest on delivery, a confirmation of the contract and the withdrawal form, on paper or by email; accept the buyer's withdrawal from the contract within 14 days of the buyer receiving the goods, without a reason, wherever the law gives that right; and deal with complaints about goods that are not as agreed. A trader who cannot meet these duties must turn off accepting orders. Oglasino does not perform these duties for the seller and does not check whether the seller has performed them.

Which country's law governs it is decided by the rules of private international law, not by these Terms: as a rule it is the law of the country where the seller lives or has its seat, and a buyer who is a consumer may also keep the protection of the consumer law of their own country. Anything that follows from sending goods across a border — customs, taxes, delivery — is for the buyer and the seller to arrange.
